Seasonal Agriculture Performance Analysis - Key Insights

1. Kharif performed best overall. It had the highest average yield (5.63 tonnes/ha) and average profit (₹178,914.65), but it also had the highest average disease/pest risk (54.47%).

2. Zaid had the weakest economic performance. Its average yield was 4.64 tonnes/ha and average profit was -₹24,804.82. It also had the highest average water use (6,419.89 m³) and the lowest water efficiency (4.41 tonnes per 1,000 m³).

3. Sugarcane and Chilli were the strongest crops by average profit. Sugarcane had an average profit of ₹817,187.99 and Chilli had ₹750,878.34.

4. Drip irrigation had the highest average yield (6.58 tonnes/ha) and average profit (₹219,626) among the irrigation methods in the dataset.

5. Water efficiency had a very strong positive correlation with yield (0.913). This shows a strong linear association in this dataset, but it does not prove that higher water efficiency alone causes higher yield.

6. Revenue had a strong positive correlation with profit (0.887). This indicates that higher revenue generally went together with higher profit in the analyzed records.

7. Fertilizer quantity and seed quality showed very weak linear relationships with yield. Their correlations with yield were about 0.000 and -0.007 respectively, so simply increasing these values does not show a clear linear yield relationship in this dataset.

Top crop-season profit combinations:
- Sugarcane + Kharif: ₹1,000,791
- Chilli + Kharif: ₹954,380
- Sugarcane + Rabi: ₹731,613
- Chilli + Rabi: ₹638,573
- Sugarcane + Zaid: ₹583,636

Recommendations:
- Use seasonal performance when planning crop selection.
- Give extra attention to Zaid-season cost, irrigation and crop selection.
- Consider profitable crops such as Sugarcane and Chilli where they are locally suitable.
- Consider efficient irrigation methods such as drip where economically feasible.
- Monitor disease and pest risk, especially during Kharif.
- Use yield, water use and profit together instead of judging performance from yield alone.
- Investigate regional and crop-season differences before making broad decisions.
